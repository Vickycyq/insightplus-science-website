How to Choose the Right Pipette Tips: A Complete Buyer's Guide for Lab Liquid Handling

Choose pipette tips with confidence by matching volume, fit, filter type, sterility, and packaging to your liquid handling workflow.

Pipette tips are a small consumable with a direct effect on liquid handling. The right choice depends on the pipette, the target volume, the sample, contamination risk, and how your team works. Use this guide to compare the practical options before ordering.

1. Match the tip to the pipette and volume

Start with the pipette model and its operating range. A tip should seat securely, seal consistently, and eject smoothly. “Universal” tips are designed for use with multiple pipette brands, but fit can vary by model. Confirm compatibility with the exact pipette in your lab before placing a large order.

Choose a tip capacity suited to the volume you dispense. Using a smaller appropriate capacity can help make low-volume work easier to control. For larger transfers, select a tip designed for the required volume and check that it reaches the vessel without touching the sides or bottom.

2. Decide whether you need a filter

Non-filtered tips are a practical option for routine transfers where aerosol carryover is not a primary concern. Filtered tips include a barrier intended to reduce aerosols and liquid from entering the pipette shaft. They are commonly selected for PCR, nucleic acid work, sensitive assays, and workflows where carryover risk matters.

A filter does not replace good pipetting practice. Use a fresh tip for each sample, follow the assay’s contamination controls, and verify that the filter and tip specification are suitable for your protocol.

3. Check length and access

Standard tips suit most tubes and plates. Extended-length tips can help reach into deep tubes, narrow vessels, or containers with tall rims while keeping the pipette body farther from the vessel. Confirm both the tip length and the opening dimensions required by your workflow.

4. Choose sterile or non-sterile packaging

For routine work, bulk bags can be convenient and space-efficient. Racked tips are easier to handle in plate-based or repetitive workflows and can be loaded into compatible multichannel pipettes. Sterile, individually sealed or boxed options can support workflows that require sterile consumables. Check the product label and documentation for the exact cleanliness claims required by your assay, such as RNase-free, DNase-free, or pyrogen-free.

6. Verify fit before scaling up

Before standardizing a tip across a lab, test representative pipettes from the models in use. Check attachment, seal, ejection, aspiration, dispensing, and access to your vessels. If your work depends on accuracy or low carryover, evaluate the tips in the actual assay workflow and retain the lot documentation.

Quick selection checklist

  • Which pipette model and volume range will you use?
  • Do you need a filtered barrier for aerosol control?
  • Would an extended-length tip improve vessel access?
  • Does the protocol require sterile tips or specific cleanliness claims?
  • Is bulk, bag, or rack/box packaging the best fit for your workflow?
  • Have you checked compatibility with the exact pipette models?
